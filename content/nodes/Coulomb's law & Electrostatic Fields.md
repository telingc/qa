## From Forces to Fields
My journey of Electrodynamics starts here, Electrostatics. To understand the fundamental question of electrodynamics, the situation of fixed charges was introduced first. Coulomb's law was stated from experiments, suggests the law that the force fixed source charges exert on test charge obeys:$$\textbf{F}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\text{qQ}}{\textbf{r}^2}\hat{\textbf{r}}$$
> **note**: In the formula, $\mathbf{r}$ stands for the vector of displacement whose magnitude is the distance between $\mathrm{q}$ and $\mathrm{Q}$, not the vector of position.

The magnitude of the force shrinks to $0$ at the infinite, and principle of superposition, which is also an empirical, revealed:$$\textbf{F}=\sum_i\textbf{F}_i=\sum_i\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\text{q}_i\text{Q}}{\textbf{r}^2}\hat{\textbf{r}}=Q\sum_i\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\text{q}_i}{\textbf{r}_i^2}\hat{\textbf{r}_i}$$it's natural to come up with the idea of electric fields, we defined electric field as:$$\mathbf{E(x)}=\sum_i\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\text{q}_i}{\textbf{r}_i^2}\hat{\textbf{r}_i},$$$$\textbf{F}=\text{Q}\textbf{E}$$the key insight of the *Coulomb's Law* for now is turning the focus of problems from forces to fields.
