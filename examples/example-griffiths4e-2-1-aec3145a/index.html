Polygon Symmetry

Let RRR be the circumradius and QQQ the test charge at the center. In the 12-sided case, the removed charge is the one at "6 o'clock"; determine the magnitude and direction of the force.

  1. Twelve equal charges, qqq , are situated at the corners of a regular 12-sided polygon. What is the net force on a test charge at the center, what if one of the charges is missing? Consider the electrostatic field at the center.
  2. Repeat the force calculation for a regular 13-sided polygon, first with every charge present and then with one charge removed; specify the removed vertex and explain the direction.
  3. Optional extension: consider a regular n-sided polygon, n≥3n\geq3n≥3, and describe both the force and the source electric field.
1. 12-sided Polygon

For the cases of regular even-sided polygon, it's quite easy to understand that for every charge in the corner, a charge at the opposite corner must exists, their net forces to the test charge in the center is same in magnitude but contradictory pointing. Thus the net force on a test charge at the center is 000. Intuitively, we can even predict that for highly symmetric even-sided polygon, the net force on a test charge in the center is 000.

Since we've already know that the net force of all stationary charges on the test charge in the center is 000, if one of the charges is missing, the rest charges have to form the contradictory pointing force so that the force is 000 for the cases above.

Thus the answer is:

3. N-sided Polygon

From our discussion above, now we can come to a conclusion that for n-sided regular polygon with charges at the corner, the net force and the field on the test charge at the center is 0\mathbf{0}0.

Bonus Topic

What if we take a special case of the regular polygon: we take the limitation that the number of sides go infinite. That is a circular charged ring, another example we'll discuss. But what if it's inside a charged sphere, the limitation case of polyhedron? The answer is, inside the charged spherical surface, the electrostatic field is always zero.
