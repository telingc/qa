Spherical Surface Using Gauss' Law

A thin spherical shell of radius RRR, centered at the origin, carries uniform surface charge density σ\sigmaσ.

  1. Use Gauss's law to determine the electric field for r<Rr<Rr<R and for r>Rr>Rr>R, including its direction.
  2. Compare the result with the direct source integration in Problem 2.7 (Spherical Surface).
1. Inside and outside

Missing Question

Which symmetry arguments let you infer a field from its flux here, and what fails if the surface charge density depends on angle?

If surface charge density depends on angle, then E\mathbf{E}E isn't angle independent, which means the surface integral couldn't use the symmetric property of E\mathbf{E}E, then we can't infer E=0\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{0}E=0 even the integral is 000. The symmetric property we need for using Gauss' law is no existing tangential field component, which is quite intuitive from the perspective considering part of the integral E⋅d a\mathbf{E}\cdot d\,\mathbf{a}E⋅da.
